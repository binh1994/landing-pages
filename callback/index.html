How it works

Three steps. Zero robots talking to your customers.

CallBack does one job: the moment a call is missed, your customer hears from you — by text, in your words, from your number.

STEP 01

The call is missed

You’re on a job, on the road, or it’s 9 PM. The caller gets no answer — the moment most shops lose them for good.

Why text-only

We deliberately don’t do voice AI. Here’s the math.

AI phone agents sound impressive in a demo. The invoice tells a different story — and for a 1–10-person shop, the text is what actually saves the job.

Voice AI receptionist

$0.10–$0.33 / minute, all-in
  • ✕ Hosting + speech-to-text + the AI model + voice + telephony all stack per minute, every minute
  • ✕ 100 calls a month at ~3 minutes each is ~300 minutes ≈ $45/mo in variable cost alone — before the plan fee
  • ✕ Spam and silent calls get billed too — one reviewer reports 100+ silent calls at $2.20 each and $300+ disputed
  • ✕ A robot that mis-books an emergency or frustrates a caller costs you the customer, not just the call

CallBack text-back

  • ✓ An SMS costs about $0.0083 — 500 texts run roughly $4–$5 total, so a flat price is actually possible
  • ✓ No minute meter: a busy month costs the same as a quiet one. No bill shock, no spam-call charges
  • ✓ Your customer talks to a text thread, then to you — a human who can actually judge an emergency
  • ✓ The caller gets an answer in seconds and a written record — address, photos and details stay in the thread

The complaint we designed around: bill shock

FAQ

Straight answers

Why no voice AI? Everyone else is adding it.+

Because of the invoice, not the demo. A voice AI call costs $0.10–$0.33 per minute once hosting, transcription, the model, the voice and telephony are stacked — roughly $45/mo in variable cost at just 100 short calls a month. At a $14–$49 price point that math only works with tight minute caps, overage charges and billed spam calls, which is exactly what the negative reviews complain about.

An SMS costs about $0.0083. Text-back is the one version of this product a small shop can sell flat, cheap, and honestly.

What happens after the automatic text?+

The conversation is yours. Replies arrive in one inbox organized by customer — you answer from your phone when your hands are free, jot a note (“gate code 4417, dog in yard”), and mark the thread Booked, Call back, or Done. Nothing else is automated behind your back.

Can it tell an emergency from a routine call?+

CallBack uses a dedicated emergency template that asks the caller for their address and a photo and flags the thread for your on-call tech. The judgement call stays with a human — you — which is the point: a burst pipe at 2 AM shouldn’t be triaged by a chatbot’s confidence score.

Who is this for — and who is it not for?+

It’s built for 1–10-person home-services shops — plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing, landscaping — where the owner is also the person on the tools. One directory counted 57 competing tools for plumbers alone in November 2026; almost all of them are racing to add voice AI. CallBack is the one that stays a text.

If you run a dispatch office with staff answering phones all day, you don’t need this. If you’re the phone, you do.

What does the trial look like?+

3 days free, then $14/mo month-to-month. Set your business profile and hours, pick or edit your three templates, and send a test missed call to your own phone to watch the text land before a real customer ever sees it. Cancel anytime — there’s no annual contract to escape.

Is $14/mo really flat? What if we have a huge month?+

Yes — flat is the product. A text costs less than a cent to send (Twilio’s published SMS rate is $0.0083 per message), so even a storm-week spike in missed calls costs us a few dollars, not a few hundred minutes. There are no overage tiers to design around, because there’s nothing expensive to meter.
